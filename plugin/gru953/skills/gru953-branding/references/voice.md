# GRU953 — voice (English reference)

This is the English voice only. Bangla is a first-class original written by the Bangla lead,
never a translation of anything here — see `references/bangla.md`. Where a surface ships
bilingually, this file gives the English half.

Source: `02_strategy/VERBAL-IDENTITY.md`. Hard facts this file will never contradict: the brand
is `GRU953` — one word, uppercase, no hyphen; it is **one person**, so never "we"; the taglines
— *Simple technology. For everyone.* and *সহজ প্রযুক্তি। সবার জন্য।* — are locked, used complete,
in both languages; the **system** (tokens, CSS, scripts) is **Apache-2.0**; the **writing** (this
guide, the guidebook, templates, docs) is **PolyForm Noncommercial 1.0.0**, which is **not**
OSI-approved — it permits noncommercial use freely, but selling it needs permission; every claim
carries its number; every limit is named out loud.

## The whole voice in one table

| | |
|---|---|
| **Sounds like** | A careful person explaining their own work to someone they respect: plain, exact, warm, never selling. |
| **Test** | Read it aloud. If a marketing department, a lawyer, or a robot could have written it, rewrite it. |
| **Never** | "we" — there is one person. In app copy, neither "I" nor "we": say what happened. |
| **Said aloud** | "Groo-nine-five-three" — one word, never spelled out, never "nine hundred and fifty-three". |

| Attribute | The rule | Never |
|---|---|---|
| **Plain** | Ordinary words, short sentences, one idea at a time. The shortest version that is still true. | Jargon dressed as sophistication. |
| **Exact** | Every claim carries its number, date or limit. Not measured? Write "unverified" and don't publish it. | Superlatives nobody can check. |
| **Warm, not chummy** | Own the problem, say what to do next. The reader is a person having a normal day. | Jokes, slang, exclamation marks, forced cheer. |
| **Steady** | Same flat calm whether the news is good, bad, or embarrassing. Apologise once, then say what you're doing. | Drama, grovelling, defensiveness, hype. |

## Sentence-level rules

1. **First mention says what GRU953 is**, immediately after the name — "GRU953 — a solo software
   studio", never "GRU953" alone. To a developer, "GRU" already means Gated Recurrent Unit, so the
   fix has to sit in the text itself.
2. **Write `GRU953`** — one word, uppercase, no hyphen, no space. Never "Gru953", "GRU 953",
   "GRU-953" in prose. `GRU-953` is the GitHub handle, not the name; lowercase `gru953` is a
   filename convention only.
3. **"I" for Aninda, "GRU953" for the studio and its products. Never "we."**
The same rule holds in Bangla — আমি, never আমরা. See `bangla.md`, where it is
stated in full; the two files used to disagree, one forbidding "we" absolutely
while the other named আমরা as the brand's own voice. In app copy, use
   neither — say what happened: "Your entry didn't save", not "we couldn't save it."
4. **Second person, always** — "you", "your".
5. **Every number-shaped claim carries its number**: speed, size, price, date. "Opens in 0.8s on a
   Redmi 9A, measured over ten runs" — not "blazing fast".
6. **Name limits in a flat, confident voice, not an apology.** A named limit builds more trust than
   a hidden one.
7. **Sorry, once, on its own line, when it's deserved. Then stop.** Follow with what you're doing
   and, where you can, a date.
8. **The system is Apache-2.0** (open source, OSI-approved). **The writing is PolyForm
   Noncommercial 1.0.0** (source-available, not OSI-approved). Never call the whole kit "open
   source" — that's true of the code, not the words.
9. Delete "simply", "just", "obviously", "of course" — they make a stuck person feel stupid.
10. Lead with the news, then the reason. "Unfortunately" is a cushion that delays bad news; cut it.

## The tone dial

Same voice, four rooms. Two settings actually move: how much warmth, how much detail.

| Situation | Warmth | Detail | The move | Never |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Welcoming someone new | High | Low | Get them to one working thing, fast | A tour of the features |
| Explaining a limitation | Medium | High | Name the limit, then the workaround | Apologising for the design |
| Apologising for a bug | Medium | High | Own it once, give a date | Excuses, or three sorries |
| Asking for money | Medium | High | State the number and what it buys | Hinting, or discounting yourself |

## Surfaces

Every example below uses an imaginary app called "Ledger". File sizes, speeds, versions, dates,
error codes and package names are invented for shape only — measure the real figure before you
publish anything.

### README opening paragraph

**Rule.** The first paragraph answers three questions and nothing else: what is this, who is it
for, what does it cost. Three sentences, max four. No badges above it, no logo wall, no "Contents"
before the reader knows what the thing is. A screenshot comes straight after, then install.

> **Ledger** keeps a record of daily takings for a one-person shop. It works offline on Android 7
> and up, in Bangla or English, and there is no account to create. Free, Apache-2.0 licensed,
> 5.3 MB.

### Release notes

**Rule.** Version and date on line one. One sentence on who should care. Then fixed headings, in
this order, dropping any that are empty: **Fixed**, **Added**, **Changed**, **Known limits**. Every
performance or size claim carries its number. Bad news goes at the top, not the bottom. No "we're
excited".

```markdown
## 2.4.1 — 8 August 2026

Update if you added entries between 2 and 6 August. This release repairs dates that 2.4.0 saved
into the wrong month.

**Fixed**
- March entries saved as April. Existing wrong dates are corrected when you next open the app.

**Known limits**
- Entries already exported to CSV are not corrected. Export again after updating.
```

### Commit messages

**Rule.** Subject line in the imperative ("add", "fix", not "added", "fixes"), under 50
characters, no full stop. If the reason isn't obvious from the code, put it in the body after a
blank line and say *why*, not *what*. Never "fix stuff", "wip", "final", or a bare emoji.

```
Fix month rollover in date picker

Months with fewer than 31 days rolled into the next month, so a
30 March entry saved as 30 April. Clamp the day to the month's
length before saving. Broken since 2.3.0.
```

### Error messages

**Rule.** Three parts, in this order: **what happened**, **why** (only if you actually know),
**what to do now**. Reassure about their data if it's safe. Never blame the person, never say
"invalid", never show a raw stack trace. A short code at the end only if it helps a search. Under
30 words.

> Couldn't save. The phone has no space left. Free up about 20 MB and tap Save again. What you
> typed is still here. (E-14)

Not: "Invalid file format." Not: "An unexpected error occurred."

### Empty and first-use states

**Rule.** Same discipline as an error message, aimed the other way: say what's true right now,
then point at the one action. Warm, short. Don't explain the studio, the licence or the roadmap
yet.

> Welcome. Ledger is ready to use. There is no account to make. Add your first sale and see
> whether it fits how you work. If it doesn't, nothing is lost.

### Issue replies

**Rule.** Four moves, in order. Thank them. Restate the problem in your own words, so they know you
understood. Say what you now know. Commit to one next step with a date, or say plainly that you
won't do it. Never leave an issue with no next step.

> Thanks for the detail, the screenshot made this quick. Confirmed: entries dated the 31st of a
> 30-day month move to the following month. It's been wrong since 2.3.0, so the fix has to repair
> old rows as well. I'm on it this week and will reply here when 2.4.1 is out.

Declining, plainly, is also a valid next step:

> Thank you, and I can see why you'd want multi-shop support. I'm not going to add it. Ledger is
> deliberately two screens, and shops brings a switcher, a settings menu and a data model that
> undoes that. The workaround that does work today: install Ledger under a second Android user
> profile, one shop each.

### Reply to a paid licence enquiry

**Rule.** Say exactly what's free, what isn't, and why, before you get anywhere near a price. The
system (colours, tokens, CSS) is free under Apache-2.0, commercial use included, no permission
needed. The writing (this guide, templates, docs) is PolyForm Noncommercial — commercial use needs
a word first. The GRU953 name and the bird mark are never for sale. Then state your rate. No
hinting, no "whatever you think is fair". Figures below are placeholders — set your own real
numbers before this is sent.

> Happy to help. The design system itself is free under Apache-2.0. You can use the colours,
> tokens and CSS in a commercial product, with no payment and no permission needed. The guidebook
> and the written templates are a different matter — those are PolyForm Noncommercial, so
> commercial use of the writing needs a quick word with me first. What isn't free at all is the
> GRU953 name and the bird mark. Those stay with the studio. If what you want is the system fitted
> to your own product and brand, that is paid work. My rate is BDT [rate] an hour, and a job like
> that is usually [range] hours. I'll quote a fixed price once I've seen your screens.

### CV line / personal statement

**Rule.** 60–80 words. No invented numbers — this is the one bio surface with zero tolerance for
them. "Complete applications" only if the CV actually lists two or more; with one, write "a
complete application"; with none yet, cut the sentence.

> Solo developer with a bias for small, finished things. I design, build and support complete
> applications on my own, from the first conversation to the bug reports. I do it for people on
> old phones and slow connections, in Bangla and English. I keep scope honest and name limits
> early. My own design system is published under Apache-2.0. I would rather ship one clear screen
> than five clever ones.

### Social post / announcement

**Rule.** Lead with what changed and its number, not with your mood. A social post is a release
note compressed to one line — it does not get to relax the rules because it's short.

> 2.5.0 adds photo receipts. Nothing else changes, and no existing data moves.

Not: "We're excited to announce our biggest update yet! 🎉"

### Invoice covering note

**Rule.** Name the invoice number, the work agreed and its date, the total with currency, the due
date, and where to ask questions. State the number without a wobble — no burying it, no "whatever
you think is fair". Figures below are placeholders.

> Invoice [GRU953-2026-014] is attached, for the work agreed on [14 July]: the offline sync and the
> two report screens. The total is BDT [amount], due [30] days from today. Bank details are on the
> invoice. Anything unclear, ask me. I'll amend it the same day.

## Before you send it

1. Is there a claim with no number, date or limit? Add one, or cut the claim.
2. Is every number a real, measured one — not an example copied from this file?
3. Is there a word from the avoid list? Replace it.
4. Read it aloud. Would you say this to a person? If not, rewrite it.
5. Is the brand written `GRU953` — one word, uppercase?
6. Does the first mention of GRU953 say what GRU953 is, right after the name?
7. Anywhere it says "we" — is that wrong? It almost always is.
8. If this surface is bilingual, has the Bangla been written as Bangla, not translated?
9. If it names a licence, is it the right one — Apache-2.0 for the system, PolyForm
   Noncommercial 1.0.0 (not OSI-approved) for the writing?

Nine checks. Call it fifteen seconds. It's the cheapest fifteen seconds in the whole kit.
