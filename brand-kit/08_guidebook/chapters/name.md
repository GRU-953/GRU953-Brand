## How it is written

**GRU953** — one word, all uppercase, no hyphen, no space, never translated.
{: .lead }

| Correct | Wrong | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| `GRU953` | `Gru953`, `gru953`, `GRU-953`, `GRU 953`, `GRU_953` | A name written five ways is five weak names instead of one strong one. |
| `GRU953` inside a Bangla sentence | জিআরইউ৯৫৩ | The name is a name. It is not transliterated, in any language. |
| `gru953` as a folder, repo or package name | using it in prose | Lowercase is a **filesystem convention only** — it is how computers prefer names, not how the brand is written. |
| `GRU-953` as the GitHub account handle | using it as the brand name | The hyphen is there because GitHub required it. It identifies the account, not the brand. |

## How it is said

**One word: “Groo-nine-five-three.”**

Not spelled out letter by letter, and not “G-R-U nine hundred and fifty-three”. When
introducing it aloud for the first time, say it and then immediately say what it is:
*“GRU953 — it’s my software studio.”* Two seconds of context prevents a minute of confusion.

## The digits stay digits

`953` is written in Latin numerals everywhere, including in Bangla text. Bengali numerals
(৯৫৩) are used for ordinary numbers in Bangla writing, but **not inside the name** — the
name is a fixed string, and changing its characters makes it a different name. The wordmark
uses tabular figures so the three digits always occupy equal width.

## The elephant in the room, measured rather than ignored

“GRU” means several other things. All four were checked properly, with sources, and here is
the honest assessment.

| “GRU” also means | The actual risk |
|---|---|
| **Gated Recurrent Unit** — a standard neural-network component | **This is the one that matters.** In machine-learning circles GRU means this and nothing else. Someone skimming a GitHub profile could read “GRU953” as the name of an AI model. It is not a legal risk — nobody owns a technical term — but it is a *reading* risk, and it lands squarely on the priority audience. |
| **GRU** — Russian military intelligence | **Very low.** The agency is never written with digits after it. The digits do the separating. |
| **Gru** — the character in *Despicable Me* | **Very low**, and mildly warm if anything. The character is sentence-case “Gru”, never “GRU953”. |
| **GRU** — São Paulo–Guarulhos airport code | **Very low.** Confined to travel contexts. |

### The decision, and the mitigation

**The name stays.** It is already established, the domains and package names are free, and no
company or trademark holds it. But the reading risk is answered, and the answer is contextual
rather than cosmetic:

> **Wherever GRU953 appears for the first time in any context, it is immediately followed by
> what it is.**

- ✅ “GRU953 — a solo software studio”
- ✅ “GRU953 · Simple technology. For everyone.”
- ✅ “Built by GRU953, a one-person studio in Bangladesh”
- ❌ “GRU953” standing entirely alone at the top of a profile with nothing after it

This is why the tagline sits directly beneath the wordmark in every lockup and every piece of
social artwork. It is not decoration. It is doing a job.

## Naming things under GRU953

As the studio grows into a product house, apps get their own names. The rules:

**The pattern is `<Name> by GRU953`** — a plain English noun, then the maker's credit. GRU953
is a parent brand over apps that carry their own names, and this is the form that says so.

- ✅ `Ledger by GRU953`
- ✅ `Tally by GRU953`, then simply `Tally` after the first mention
- ❌ `GRU-Ledger`, `Gru953Ledger`, `953 Ledger`, `LedgerGRU`
- ❌ `Ledgerly`, `Ledgerify`, `Ledgr` — invented words age badly and are hard to say in two languages

**One exception.** When a name is too generic to stand alone in a list — `Notes`, `Files`,
`Camera` — put the studio first instead: `GRU953 Notes`. Never both forms in the same
document.

**A good product name here is:** a real word, sayable by a Bangla speaker and an English
speaker without hesitation, spellable after hearing it once, and descriptive of what the
thing does. **A bad one is:** a misspelling, a dropped vowel, a suffix like *-ly* or *-ify*, or
anything that needs explaining before it can be used.

Check before committing: is the `.com` or `.dev` free, is the npm and PyPI package name free,
and does the word mean something unfortunate in Bangla?
